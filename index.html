How it works
  1. Every Monday, one security is picked at random from the full universe and 1 share is bought at that day's close.
  2. Dividends are reinvested on the ex-date. Reinvested shares are tracked separately.
  3. Every 26 weeks, every position is ranked by total return. All but the top 12 are sold.
  4. Each of the 12 survivors doubles its purchased shares. Reinvested dividend shares don't count toward the doubling.
  5. Benchmark: the same dollars, on the same days, put into SPY (dividends reinvested).

Picks are drawn by a scheduled job using a cryptographic RNG and committed publicly to picks.json. Prices from Yahoo Finance, split-adjusted, refreshed after each close. A model portfolio, not financial advice.

What is the Random Stock Picker?

A free tool that picks one stock or ETF at random from every security listed on NASDAQ, NYSE, NYSE Arca, NYSE American (AMEX) and Cboe BZX. That's 12,302 securities, refreshed from NASDAQ's official symbol directory. Every pick links to its live chart, company profile, fundamentals and SEC filings. Each pick also has its own link, so you can share it.

Is it really random?

Yes. Picks use your browser's Web Crypto API (crypto.getRandomValues()), the same randomness source used to generate encryption keys. Nothing is weighted by popularity, market cap or sponsorship.

Can a random stock picker beat the market?

Burton Malkiel famously argued that a blindfolded monkey throwing darts could pick a portfolio as well as the experts. Later backtests of random portfolios found many beat a cap-weighted index. Researchers mostly credit that to tilts toward smaller and value stocks, not to luck. The Random Strategy panel tests a version of this in public, one random pick a week, against the S&P 500.
